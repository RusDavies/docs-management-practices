# Writing Style Guidance

## Public Brand and Representation Positioning

These management-practice docs may support public writing, personal-brand material, employment positioning, and professional credibility. Example names and personas should therefore be handled deliberately.

The goal is affirmative representation without crude protected-trait mapping.

Use this positioning:

- Positive, competent, leadership, recovery, coaching, and good-judgment examples should intentionally include women and culturally diverse names/personas.
- Negative, failure, dysfunction, risk, and anti-pattern examples should avoid making women or diverse personas carry the problem case.
- White male-coded names should not be the default hero, beneficiary, rescuer, or assumed competent actor.
- Do not create a simplistic identity-to-virtue or identity-to-fault table. The point is professional optics and inclusive representation, not stereotype inversion with a nicer hat.
- Prefer human, engaging examples over sterile role-only examples when readability matters.
- Use role-only examples when a named persona would distract from the point, create avoidable demographic baggage, or increase publication risk.
- Review the full document for aggregate patterns, not just each sentence in isolation.

## Practical Example Patterns

Safer negative/systemic example:

> By the time anyone noticed, one senior engineer had become the undocumented approval path for every major release.

Positive named example:

> Amara, a newly promoted team lead, made the disagreement explicit before the planning meeting drifted into fake consensus.

Positive named example:

> Priya rebuilt trust with the customer by saying plainly what had failed, what would change, and when the next update would land.

Neutral named example:

> Pat owned the migration plan, but the decision rights were never written down.

## Review Checklist

Before publishing, check whether the examples accidentally imply that:

- men are the default leaders
- white male personas are the default heroes
- women or culturally diverse personas are disproportionately used for mistakes, conflict, dysfunction, or risk
- representation appears tokenistic rather than natural
- the writing could be clipped or misread as anti-DEI resentment
- the examples are so sanitized that they stop sounding human
