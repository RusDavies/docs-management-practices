# Publication Decision Gates

Some sections may be useful for internal reasoning but risky for public, employment-facing, or personal-brand publication. Do not publish those sections by default. Pass them through a deliberate decision gate.

## Decision Gate: DEI, Equity, and Pipeline Asymmetry Content

Use this gate for any section discussing DEI, equity, affirmative action, demographic representation, candidate-pool asymmetry, sex/race/gender patterns, or hiring-stage correction.

### Default Status

Draft/internal by default.

Do not publish unless explicitly approved after review.

### Publish / Exclude Decision

Before publication, decide one of:

- **Publish as written** — acceptable only if the section is constructive, balanced, and unlikely to read as anti-DEI resentment.
- **Publish after revision** — keep the useful management point but soften, reframe, source, or move sensitive claims.
- **Exclude from public version** — retain internally, but remove from public/personal-brand material.
- **Replace with safer framing** — discuss early pipeline development, fair assessment, and inclusive opportunity without engaging the contested DEI framing directly.

### Review Questions

- Could this be clipped or read as anti-DEI positioning?
- Does it sound like resentment about protected groups rather than concern about fair systems?
- Is the section useful to managers, or is it mostly culture-war shrapnel?
- Are claims framed as observations and risks rather than universal accusations?
- Does the section support constructive management action?
- Would a recruiter, executive, HR leader, or diverse reader see this as thoughtful rather than hostile?
- Is publication worth the brand risk?

### Safer Public Framing

When in doubt, prefer:

> Candidate-pool asymmetry often forms long before hiring. Responsible organizations should broaden early exposure, mentorship, access, and retention while keeping hiring-stage evaluation transparent, role-relevant, and fair to individuals.

This preserves the management point without wandering into a swamp wearing lead boots.
