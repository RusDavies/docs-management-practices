# Stale-Content Review List

## Purpose

Find, triage, repair, deprecate, archive, or delete content that may be wrong, misleading, duplicated, ownerless, or unsafe for reuse.

## Review Scope

- Knowledge base / repository / domain:
- Review owner:
- Review date:
- Review trigger:

## Review List

| Content / link | Owner | Last reviewed | Staleness signal | Risk | Decision | Action owner | Due date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |  |  | update / deprecate / archive / delete / keep |  |  |

## Staleness Signals

- owner missing or departed
- review date overdue
- conflicting source exists
- linked system, process, product, policy, vendor, or customer changed
- support or operations reports repeated confusion
- AI retrieval produced weak or wrong citations
- page is frequently viewed but feedback is negative
- page is not viewed but remains marked authoritative

## Risk Rating

| Rating | Meaning | Default action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Could cause customer harm, security/privacy/compliance issue, operational failure, or major rework | Fix or restrict immediately |
| Medium | Could cause repeated confusion, inefficiency, inconsistent decisions, or support burden | Schedule update or deprecation |
| Low | Mostly historical or low-impact | Archive, label, or leave with context |

## Usage Notes

- Review dangerous stale content before cosmetic cleanup.
- Do not let stale approved content remain indistinguishable from current guidance.
- Preserve historical records when useful, but label them clearly. Archaeology is fine; accidental policy necromancy is not.

## Example

A legacy support article still ranks highly in search but describes a retired workflow. Mark it deprecated, link the replacement article, remove it from AI retrieval, and update support macros that still point to it.
