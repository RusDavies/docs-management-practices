# Change Definition Brief

## Purpose

Define a change clearly enough that sponsors, delivery teams, managers, affected groups, support teams, and customers can understand what is changing, why, what is not changing, and how success will be judged.

## Change Statement

> We are changing [current thing] to [future thing] for [reason/outcome]. This affects [groups/processes/systems]. It does not affect [important exclusions]. Success means [observable result].

## Usage Notes

- Use this before writing a communication plan. If the change cannot be defined, announcing it louder is not strategy.
- Be explicit about exclusions; many avoidable arguments come from people guessing what else is secretly changing.
- Keep the brief alive until rollout decisions are made, then archive the final version as decision evidence.

## Example

A finance team moving from spreadsheet-based approvals to a governed procurement tool would define affected spend categories, approver roles, exception paths, reporting changes, legacy spreadsheet retirement, and success signals such as cycle time, audit evidence completeness, and reduced approval ambiguity.
