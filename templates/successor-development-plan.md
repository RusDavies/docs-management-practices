# Successor Development Plan

## Purpose

Create a practical development plan for a person who may need to assume a critical role, responsibility, relationship, decision right, or specialist capability in the future.

This is not a coronation certificate. It is a structured way to close readiness gaps, test capability through real work, and reduce succession risk without pretending humans are interchangeable printer cartridges.

## Usage Notes

- Use for successors, deputies, backup owners, critical specialists, founders/executives, relationship owners, and operational roles with continuity exposure.
- Keep the plan developmental and evidence-based. Do not promise a role unless that decision has actually been made by the right humans.
- Review alongside succession risk, role coverage, knowledge-transfer, and access/authority gaps.
- Be careful with confidentiality and fairness; succession planning should reduce risk, not become palace intrigue with better headings.

## Example

A potential engineering lead successor may shadow design reviews, lead one planning cycle, own a bounded architecture decision, join incident retrospectives, document service ownership, and receive feedback from product, security, and operations stakeholders before being marked ready soon.
