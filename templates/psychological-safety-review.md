# Psychological Safety Review

## Purpose

Assess whether people can ask questions, admit mistakes, raise risks, disagree, and ask for help while still maintaining high standards.

## Evidence Prompts

- What risks or problems surfaced early recently?
- What risks or problems surfaced too late?
- Who speaks in meetings, and who does not?
- How do leaders respond to being challenged?
- What happens after someone makes an honest mistake?
- What happens after repeated poor behaviour or low standards?
- Where might fear, embarrassment, status, or retaliation suppress useful truth?

## Decisions

- What behaviour will leaders model differently?
- What meeting or decision norm will change?
- What consequence or accountability practice needs repair?
- What will be communicated back to the team?

## Usage Notes

- Psychological safety is not comfort at all costs. It is permission to tell the truth about the work.
- Do not use this review to identify “unsafe people” by algorithm or sentiment score.
- Pair this with manager expectations and culture health reviews.

## Example

If engineers do not challenge launch dates until after incidents, inspect whether previous dissent was ignored, punished, or drowned out by optimism cosplay.
