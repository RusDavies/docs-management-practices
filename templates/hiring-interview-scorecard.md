# Hiring / Interview Scorecard

Use this scorecard to evaluate candidates against role-relevant evidence using a consistent, structured process.

Do not use it to launder vague impressions into official-looking objectivity. If the evidence is weak, say the evidence is weak. The table is not magic; it is merely less ridiculous than comparing “vibes.”

## Ratings

Use ratings as decision aids, not as a substitute for evidence.

Suggested scale:

- **1 — Does not meet evidence threshold**
- **2 — Partially meets evidence threshold; material concerns remain**
- **3 — Meets evidence threshold**
- **4 — Strongly meets evidence threshold**
- **5 — Exceptional evidence for this criterion**

## Bias and Fairness Check

Before making a recommendation, check:

- Were all candidates assessed against the same core role criteria?
- Did we separate must-have requirements from preferences?
- Are concerns tied to observable evidence and role needs?
- Did we avoid using “culture fit” as a proxy for comfort or similarity?
- Were accommodations, accessibility, language, time-zone, or context factors handled fairly?
- Did we overvalue confidence, polish, pedigree, familiarity, or interview performance where the role requires different evidence?
- Did we broaden sourcing without lowering the role-relevant evidence bar?
- Are we reducing any candidate to a demographic symbol rather than evaluating individual evidence?

## Retention and Privacy

Handle scorecards as sensitive personnel records.

- Store only where authorized.
- Follow applicable retention and privacy rules.
- Avoid irrelevant personal information.
- Do not include protected-class speculation or medical/family assumptions.
- Keep rejected-candidate notes factual, respectful, and role-relevant.
